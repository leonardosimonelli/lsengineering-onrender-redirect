Preliminary wing design: NACA airfoils, spars and FEM

A practical workflow for screening wing concepts before detailed verification, testing and independent validation.

1. Define the design envelope

Start with mass, design loads, airspeed, altitude, wing span and chord, structural materials, constraints and the overall center of gravity. Include relevant off-design conditions and uncertainties rather than checking only a nominal point.

2. Compare NACA airfoils carefully

NACA designations describe airfoil geometry, not guaranteed aircraft performance. Compare aerodynamic coefficients at appropriate Reynolds numbers and angles of attack. Two-dimensional section data cannot substitute for three-dimensional wing behavior.

3. Consider lift and pitching moment together

Check whether each candidate produces sufficient lift and examine the aerodynamic moment about the overall center of gravity across the intended operating range. Center-of-pressure location can vary with incidence, making a single-point optimum misleading.

4. Size spars and ribs

Transfer distributed aerodynamic loads to a structural model with justified supports and attachment conditions. Consider bending, shear, torsion, deflection and local stability as appropriate. Rib lightening holes require geometric clearance and stress concentration checks.

5. Validate FEM results

Mesh refinement cannot compensate for incorrect boundary conditions or loads. Compare against independent calculations, test mesh convergence and inspect highly loaded attachments. Document assumptions, load cases and uncertainty.

6. Keep preliminary design distinct from certification

Concept screening is not approval for manufacture or flight. Detailed engineering verification, physical testing and applicable regulatory compliance remain separate requirements.
